# How to organize a family with tududi

A family is not a team, and nobody in the house has a sprint. There is no manager, though there is a lot of managing. Bills arrive on the same day every month. The school sends forms home in a backpack, and someone has to find them, sign them and send them back. Plants die when nobody waters them.

Most of this lives in one head, usually the head of whoever noticed first. The rest of the house finds out when something is late.

The stories parents tell about this are boringly similar. A birthday is next week, and the other parent only thinks about it when someone asks them for a present. A form takes five minutes to sign, but first someone has to find it, remember the due date and make sure it goes back. And the sentence that comes up in almost every thread: they would have done it, if I had asked.

Asking is the job. By the time you ask, you have already noticed, decided when, and turned it into a request. The other person gets the easy five minutes at the end.

A tool can't notice for anyone. What it can do is take the list out of one head and put it where everyone it concerns can see it. I wanted that to happen inside the tool I already use for my own work, not in a separate family app with chore charts and points.

Everything below runs on **tududi Cloud**. Sign up at [tududi.com](https://tududi.com), and that account becomes the one that holds the house. There is nothing to install.

I use fictional names here, so no real family ends up in a screenshot. The Parkers: Alex and Maria, two kids, Emma and Leo, and Grandma Helen, who picks the kids up on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

## Start with People

Open **People** from the sidebar. Everyone you can hand a task to lives here. The page has three tabs, **All**, **Members** and **Contacts**, so you can see who has an account and who is only a name on a list.

A **member** has an account. They can sign in, see what is theirs and tick it off.

A **contact** is someone you only keep track of: a grandparent, a plumber, the football coach. A contact is a label you can put on a task. Nobody is notified and nothing is shared.

![The People page with the Parker family](family-post/people.png)

Grandma Helen is a contact. She will never open tududi, and she doesn't need to. When Alex gives her the school pickup, nothing buzzes on her phone. Someone still has to call her. But everyone can see that the job is covered, and by whom.

## Share the Projects That Matter

Alex and Maria both run the house, so the **Household** and **School** projects are shared between them. Shared projects appear under **Projects** on both accounts. Sharing is per project, so the summer trip to Crete can stay private until it is less of a mess.

![The projects list for the Parker household](family-post/projects.png)

A project list is a good place to split the work. One parent can own school, doctors and clothes while the other owns the dentist and swimming. Whoever owns a project keeps it, and the other person can read and write, so nothing is hidden from either of them.

## Assign, Don't Remind

This is the part that changes the house.

Every task can be assigned to a person. When you assign a task to a member, they are notified and they see that task, even if the project isn't shared with them. A child with an assigned task sees that task and nothing else: no electricity bill, no other projects.

The fastest way is the **Inbox** capture box. Type the task the way you would say it out loud:

> Pick up Leo at 12:30 on Wednesday, early release @Grandma +School

![The capture box with a date, a person and a project](family-post/add-box.png)

As you type, `@Grandma` and `+School` turn into a person chip and a project chip. "Wednesday" becomes the due date, and all three show up before you press Enter. Choose **Task** under *Add to* and it goes straight into the task list. Leave it on **Inbox** and it waits there until you sort it.

This doesn't fix noticing. Someone still has to read the school email. But whoever reads it writes it down and hands it over in the same sentence, and it's out of their head.

Reminding is a job. Assigning is a sentence.

## The Everyone Board

Once you share a project or assign someone a task, **Everyone** shows up in the sidebar.

![The Everyone board with tasks for each family member](family-post/everyone.png)

One column per person: you first, then each person you share work with. Each column is grouped by Overdue, Today, Tomorrow, This week and No date, so the things due later stay out of the way until they get closer. Contacts appear too, which is how Grandma Helen's Wednesday pickup sits in her own column and nobody stands at the gate wondering who was supposed to come.

This is the screen for Sunday evening. Not to check on anyone, just to see whether the week is balanced. If one column has the overdue bill, the field trip form and the passports, and another has a car wash, the board says it without anyone having to.

## What Is Left Out

No points. No streaks for chores. No leaderboard between siblings.

Recurring tasks work for the house the same way they work for anything else: the recycling every Monday, the plants every evening. Habits are there if someone wants to track their own reading. tududi doesn't turn the family into a game, because the moment it does, the game becomes the work.

## Getting the House In

tududi Cloud is priced per person. You pay for yourself and for each member you add, and a member without an email counts the same as a partner with one, because they get the same column. There is a 14-day money-back guarantee if it turns out not to suit you.

Sign up at [tududi.com](https://tududi.com), add the people who live with you, and give the first task away.

The house doesn't need a system that demands attention. It needs one that holds the small things so that the people in it don't have to.
